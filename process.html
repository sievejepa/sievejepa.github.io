SIEVE · how it works

A learner that eats one factor at a time

SIEVE never sees a label. It builds a ladder of pairs, each rung redrawing one more factor, and takes one bite per rung: compare, subtract what it already ate, sieve, keep the four directions that are left. Step through it below. The numbers are real (seed 0 of the 7-factor synthetic run, LeJEPA encoder, whitened).

What makes the bite harder

On the synthetic world each bite is clean. On real images the encoder decides how much the learner can get:

The factor isn't in the embeddingNothing to sieve. Where the full-embedding R² for a factor is about zero (the ResNet run's gripper, yaw, position, colour), its block recovers nothing.
The factor is spread outWith whitening, a PickCube factor occupies hundreds of directions, so a mouth of rank 1–3 catches almost nothing; ranks of 12–16 or more are needed. Without whitening, 1–5 directions are enough.
The factor is entangledSome blocks respond more to pairs that hold their own factor fixed than to pairs that vary it, so they pick up other factors and leak.
